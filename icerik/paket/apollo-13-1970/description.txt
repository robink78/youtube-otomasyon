On April 13, 1970, an oxygen tank exploded aboard Apollo 13, 200,000 miles from Earth. Its heater switches had been rated for 28 volts while ground equipment supplied 65. The failure mechanism: thermostat switches welded shut by overvoltage, overheating the tank's wiring insulation.

Failure chain: Switches rated 28 v, ground supply 65 v → Switches weld shut during detanking → Heater cooks wire insulation → Spark in pure oxygen in flight → Tank ruptures — mission aborted
