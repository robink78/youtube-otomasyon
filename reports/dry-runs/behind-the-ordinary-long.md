**THE_QUESTION** — Start with the detail itself. That tiny jeans pocket was not designed for loose change. It is a surviving piece of nineteenth-century workwear. Levi Strauss and Company calls it the watch pocket. Pocket watches were common, so the small pouch carried and protected one. It appeared on the original four-pocket riveted waist overalls in 1873. The second back pocket arrived in 1901, making that the actual fifth pocket. So this tiny pocket is a working trace of the original jeans design. The small front-right pocket on classic Levi's jeans is called a watch pocket.

**ORIGIN_CONTEXT** — Its history begins with a practical problem. The small watch pocket was part of the original riveted waist overalls introduced in 1873. Levi Strauss and Jacob Davis received a United States patent for improving the fastening of pocket openings in 1873. The original trousers were known as waist overalls. The original waist overalls had four pockets in total. Those four pockets were two large front pockets, the watch pocket, and one back pocket. The original waist overalls also used a button fly, copper rivets, blue denim, and arcuate stitching.

**THE_PROBLEM** — That problem shaped the design. Copper rivets strengthened pocket openings and helped keep them from ripping.

**THE_EXPLANATION** — The documentation makes the purpose clearer. The governing physics: The small pouch was designed as a watch pocket for carrying and protecting a pocket watch. The watch pocket was intended to hold a pocket watch, a common possession in the late nineteenth century. Levi Strauss and Company describes the watch pocket as protection for a pocket watch.

**SURPRISING_DETAIL** — But one detail complicates the familiar story. It is often called the fifth pocket, but the second back pocket is the actual fifth pocket in the documented design sequence. The tiny front pocket is sometimes called the fifth pocket, but it was part of the original four-pocket arrangement. A second back pocket was added in 1901.

**REAL_WORLD_CONSEQUENCE** — The design did not stop changing there. The original watch pocket survived as the five-pocket jean design evolved. During the Second World War, rivets were removed from the watch pocket to save metal. The watch-pocket rivets were restored after the wartime change.

**FINAL_PAYOFF** — Now the opening question has a precise answer. The back pocket added in 1901 is the actual fifth pocket in the documented design sequence. The watch pocket remains part of the familiar five-pocket jean design.
